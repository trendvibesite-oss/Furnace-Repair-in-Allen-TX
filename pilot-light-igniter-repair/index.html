Types of Furnace Ignition Systems Serviced

Hot Surface Igniters (HSI)

Modern furnaces use silicon nitride or carbide elements that glow white-hot at 2,000°F. Over time, thermal fatigue causes internal element fractures.

Standing Pilots & Thermocouples

Older furnaces rely on a continuous gas flame heating a copper thermocouple to produce 25–30 millivolts, holding the safety valve open.

Intermittent Pilot Ignition

Electronic spark ignites a pilot flame only when heat is called, saving fuel while maintaining reliable ignition safety.

Flame Rectification Sensors

Stainless steel sensor rods verify combustion using microamp electrical currents; cleaning soot from the sensor restores immediate ignition.
