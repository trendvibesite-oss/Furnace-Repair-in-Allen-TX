How Airflow Restrictions Harm Your Heating System

High-Limit Safety Lockouts

Choked airflow causes heat exchanger temperatures to spike above safe thresholds, opening safety limit switches repeatedly.

Premature Heat Exchanger Cracking

Continuous thermal cycling between extreme heat and rapid cooldown causes metal seams to fracture prematurely.

Blower Motor Overheating

Restricted return ducts force blower motors to work harder, drawing excessive electrical amperage and burning out capacitors.

Higher Monthly Heating Bills

Restricted airflow forces your furnace to run for extended cycles to satisfy thermostat setpoints, wasting energy.

Frequently Asked Questions

For standard 1-inch filters, MERV 8 to 11 provides excellent filtration without creating excessive static pressure. Avoid 1-inch MERV 13+ filters unless your ductwork is specifically designed for high airflow resistance.

In most North Texas homes, filters are located either in return air grilles mounted in ceilings/hallways or inside a filter track attached directly to the attic furnace or air handler.

Check 1-inch filters every 30 days and replace them every 30 to 60 days during active winter heating. Thicker 4-inch or 5-inch media filters typically last 6 to 9 months.
